Relevance Realization: the one idea under the whole architecture

Why the interesting parts of this codebase are four copies of the same faculty at four timescales.

The win: after this lesson, when a feature request lands you can say which of the four RR instances it touches — Interruption, Urge, Recall, Capture — and why "just add a score for it" is usually the wrong first move.

The problem RR answers

At any moment, a Persona in a Room faces an intractable space: everything ever said, every memory, every possible reply. Deciding what matters cannot be done by rule — any rule for "check what's relevant" must itself decide what's relevant to check. This is the frame problem, and it's why Vervaeke, Lillicrap & Richards (2012) argue relevance can never be computed from a fixed formula — it must be realized: continuously self-organized by balancing opposing constraints, the way an economy sets prices without anyone computing them.

Two properties follow, and both are load-bearing in this repo:

  • Opponent processing. RR is implemented as tension between pulls that are each sometimes right — explore⇄exploit, general⇄special. You keep the tension structural; you don't average it away.
  • Self-organization. What's relevant reshapes itself through use: attended things strengthen, ignored things fade. No central scorer.

Same faculty, four clocks. When you see "salience" in the stop-signal race and "salience" in memory ranking, that's not name reuse — it's the claim that interruption and remembering are the same judgement at different timescales.

Worked example 1: self-organization in ten lines

backend/Services/Memory/SalienceMath.cs is the smallest complete expression of RR's self-organizing half:

salience = weight × decay(now − captured) + use_bonus(recall_count, last_recalled)
  • weight — Capture's forward bet: "how much would this stick, a day later?" (emitted by the extractor, not a rule).
  • decay — 7-day half-life on wall-clock time: the landscape flattens on its own.
  • use_bonus — increments only when the Decision phase picks the memory, never on mere surfacing in the prompt. Attention strengthens; exposure doesn't. That single rule is what makes the landscape self-organizing rather than a popularity counter.

Worked example 2: opponent processing kept structural

ADR 0015 had to merge two pulls: memories relevant to this beat (graph-walk from cast + concepts) and memories that are simply recent. The obvious move — one formula, weight × decay × relevance_boost — was rejected. Instead: a quota union, ~5 slots for the relevant arm, the rest recent, each arm internally salience-ranked.

The stated reason is the architectural lesson of this whole course: a merged formula invents a tunable cross-arm weight with no eval harness to tune it against, and lets a hot topic starve everything else. The quota keeps the two pulls in structural tension — opponent processing — and guarantees a serendipity budget of recent-but-unrelated memories. Same pattern in UrgeMath.CalculateNetPressure: pull (mention, question, cold-open, chaos) minus push (round penalty, self-dominance penalty), listed as separate named forces, not folded into one learned score.

Heuristic for your future ADRs: when two selection pressures compete, reach for a structural tension (quota, race, separate named terms) before a numerical one (one blended formula). Graduate to a formula only when an eval harness exists to tune it.

Check yourself

Answer from memory before clicking — the retrieval is the point.

1. Feature request: "Personas should bring up an old promise when the same topic resurfaces weeks later." Which RR instance does this primarily touch?

Recall: the promise already exists as a memory; the work is making it surface when the topic anchors match — the relevant arm of ADR 0015's quota union is exactly this path.

2. Why did ADR 0015 reject the single merged formula weight × decay × relevance_boost?

A blended weight with no eval harness is an untunable dial, and a hot topic can starve the window. The quota keeps the pulls in structural tension — the opponent-processing shape.

3. When does a Recollection's recall_count increment?

Pick only — "attention strengthens, exposure doesn't." This is what makes the salience landscape self-organizing instead of a counter of prompt appearances.

4. Why can't relevance be computed by a fixed rule, per Vervaeke?

The frame problem: any rule for checking relevance must itself select what to check — infinite regress. Hence relevance is realized by self-organizing opponent processes, not computed.
